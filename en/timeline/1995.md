## Memory landmarks of the year

- **A Chinese Odyssey**（Stephen Chow / Athena Chu）（Film · Jan）：Jeffrey Lau's two-part 1995 comedy starring Stephen Chow as the Monkey King torn between nonsense and love. A box-office flop at release, later canonized via campus BBS and bootleg VCDs as a cult masterpiece of love and regret.
- **The Legend of Sword and Fairy (DOS)**（Softstar (Taiwan) · Yao Chuang-Hsien）（Games · Jul）：Softstar's July 1995 DOS wuxia RPG; Li Xiaoyao and Zhao Linger's tragedy became a template for Chinese-language single-player storytelling and launched the golden age of domestic PC games. Note: dedup against any existing PAL entry.
- **In the Heat of the Sun**（Jiang Wen / Xia Yu）（Film · Aug）：Jiang Wen's 1995 directorial debut, from Wang Shuo's novella, recalling a Beijing teen's sun-blasted Cultural-Revolution summer. Teen lead Xia Yu became Venice's youngest Best Actor; grossed ~50M RMB and topped Time's world films of the year.
- **Rumble in the Bronx**（Jackie Chan）（Film · Jan）：Jackie Chan's 1995 New Year action film, set in New York: his stuntless rooftop leaps won Hollywood's respect. ~95M RMB to top mainland box office and ~$82.3M worldwide—the first HK blockbuster imported to the mainland and the film that introduced the 'New Year movie.'
- **Empress Wu Zetian**（Liu Xiaoqing）（TV）：Chen Jialin's 1995 historical epic with Liu Xiaoqing playing Empress Wu Zetian from teenage girl to aged sovereign across decades. Its lavish production and Liu's casting sparked huge debate; a landmark of imperial Chinese TV drama.
- **Jacky Cheung — True Love (New Songs + Selections)**（Jacky Cheung (PolyGram)）（Music）：Jacky Cheung's 1995 Mandarin compilation; A Thousand Sad Reasons and I Waited Till the Flowers Withered became KTV staples. Set a long-standing mainland sales record; he took the World Music Award for top-selling Chinese artist that year.
- **Toy Story**（Pixar）（Film · Nov）：The first fully CGI animated feature, celebrating the friendship of Woody and Buzz Lightyear.
- **Death of Teresa Teng**（Teresa Teng）（Events · May）：On May 8, 1995, the beloved singer Teresa Teng died of an acute asthma attack in Chiang Mai, Thailand, aged just 42. Voice of classics like 'The Moon Represents My Heart,' she was mourned across the Chinese-speaking world; 'wherever there are Chinese, there is Teng's voice.'
